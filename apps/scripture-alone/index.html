01Reading

The whole Bible, offline

The American Standard Version is on your device from the moment the app installs. The Berean Standard Bible and the King James Version are one download away.

Learn more

02Study

Study mode

Tap a verse and the Study panel follows, with ranked cross-references and classic commentary from John Calvin, John Gill and Jamieson‑Fausset‑Brown.

Learn more

03Notes & sermon slides

Camera notes

Photograph the sermon slide and on-device text recognition starts your note, linked to every passage on it. The photo is discarded unless you keep it.

Learn more

04Family

Keepsake Bible

Your highlights and notes, with a dedication, in one file your family can open and read just as you marked it — a digital “Dad's Bible” to hand down.

Learn more

05Apple Watch

Widgets and Apple Watch

Verse of the Day widgets for your Home and Lock Screens, and an Apple Watch app that reads offline. Your notes follow you through your own private iCloud.

Yes, in a closed beta on Google Play: a native app for Android phones and tablets, with its own Wear OS app. Your highlights and notes stay on the device, since there's no iCloud on Android.

To join, join the beta group, then opt in on Google Play with the same Google account and install it from there. If Google Play says the test isn't available yet, the first build is still in Google's review, so try again in a day or two.

No Google Play? Download the APK from GitHub, with every translation and study tool built in. It doesn't update itself, and it can't be updated by the Google Play version, so read the notes there first.

Scripture Alone can open a USFM file, a DRM-free ePub or a Bible PDF you own, and it can fetch the free catalogue published by eBible.org when you ask it to, with your own language first — nothing is downloaded unless you tap. What you import is yours alone: it follows you to your other devices through your own private iCloud, and copying and sharing from it follow the publisher’s own published terms. This is an interim arrangement: requests to license the major modern translations are with their publishers, and if those are granted the translations will simply be in the app, and this may go away.
