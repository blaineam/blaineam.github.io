Everything people ask about Scripture Alone, from what it costs (nothing) to where its translations come from.

Translations

It's a careful, word-for-word translation in the public domain, so it can be given away freely — the Berean Standard Bible and King James Version are included for the same reason. Translations like the CSB, ESV, NKJV and NASB need publisher licenses, which we're requesting.

Yes. Scripture Alone can open a USFM file, a DRM-free ePub or a Bible PDF you own, and it can fetch the free catalogue published by eBible.org when you ask it to, with your own language first — nothing is downloaded unless you tap. What you import is yours alone: it follows you to your other devices through your own private iCloud, and copying and sharing from it follow the publisher’s own published terms. This is an interim arrangement: requests to license the major modern translations are with their publishers, and if those are granted the translations will simply be in the app, and this may go away.

Until they can be included in the app, two files are known to import well. For the ESV, the ePub edition of the Reformation Study Bible. For the CSB, the pew Bible PDF its publisher offers. Open either with Import a File… in Translations.
