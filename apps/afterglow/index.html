Afterglow

Phone VR, done properly.

Your photos and videos in 3D. Panoramas and 360 photos all around you. A WebXR browser your hands can reach into. Games in your own room. All on the iPhone in your pocket and a viewer you print.

Get it on the App Store Source on GitHub

Free and open source (Apache-2.0). Built for the iPhone 18 Pro Max.

Cardboard showed a phone could be a headset. Afterglow finishes the thought: real head tracking, your hands, your room, and an interface you can actually read through a lens.

Your room

Windows that stay where you put them

ARKit gives Afterglow six degrees of freedom, so you can lean in, step round and look behind. The cameras pass your room through, and glass windows float in it with a launcher and a dock at your feet.

Hand tracking runs on the Neural Engine. Pinch to select, grab to move, point to aim. No controller, nothing to charge.

Photos

Your library, in three dimensions

Spatial photos and videos from an iPhone or Vision Pro play back in stereo. Panoramas wrap round you. 360 photos become the room itself, found automatically across your whole library.

Albums, a filmstrip, a scrub bar, and projection you can switch when a file lies about what it is.

Browser

A WebXR browser your hands reach into

Browse flat pages in a floating window, then enter immersive WebXR sessions with your hands as the controllers. Pages get head pose, hand joints and pinch events, the way a headset would give them.

A native start page, a keyboard you can type on through the lens, and a quiet exit: double-tap the viewer.

Places

Twenty-four places to be

Pick a photosphere and the room is gone: a mountain lake, a desert at dusk, a city at night. Bring your own 360 photos and they sit alongside the bundled ones.

Places are stills, with no server behind them. Everything renders on the phone.

Games

Twelve games in your own room

Paint in the air with glowing tubes. Putt eighteen holes of mini golf at your feet. Ride a coaster on a course that is new every time. Shoot blobs, vacuum the floor, wake a toy box, open a portal. Stack a tower on your real table, grow a glowing garden, keep a ball in orbit with your palms, and drive a neon road by looking where you want to go.

Rhythm plays to real music. Afterglow measures the tempo of your own tracks, and for songs from Apple Music it looks the beat up so the game lands on it.

Song tempo data for the rhythm game is provided by audiometa.io.

From inside

See what you'd see

The interface and every experience, from where you stand. Each picture is one eye without the lens warp, unless it says through the lenses.

Rendered by the app itself in the iPhone Simulator, which has no camera, so its synthwave surroundings stand in for your room. On a phone these float in your real room, in stereo.

The viewer

Print it. Three parts, no electronics.

Afterglow is tuned for a viewer you print yourself: a shell sized to the iPhone 18 Pro Max, a lens tray, and a cover that leaves the cameras clear so passthrough and hand tracking can see.

  • Ready to print on MakerWorld, with OpenSCAD source in the repository.
  • A short bill of materials: two lenses and a strap.
  • Tap the viewer to select. Double-tap to leave an immersive session.
Print it from MakerWorld

An Apple Watch can also act as a controller: wrist taps and the crown, over a thin connection to the phone.

Yours, and open

Afterglow runs on the phone. Your photos, your room and your hands never leave it. The whole app is open source, so you can read exactly what it does.

There are no accounts and no analytics. The only network calls are the ones you make: the pages you browse, and a tempo lookup for a song you chose. A web page gets your hand positions only if you allow it, site by site.

Fork it, build it, change it. The source, the viewer files and the test harness are all in the repository.

Song tempo data by audiometa.io. Head and hand tracking are Apple's ARKit and Vision frameworks. The bundled photospheres are the author's own.

Frequently Asked Questions

Which phones does it run on?

It is built and tested on the iPhone 18 Pro Max, with the printed viewer sized to it. Other recent Pro iPhones with LiDAR should work, but the viewer and the lens geometry are tuned for the 18 Pro Max.

Is it on the App Store?

Yes, as Afterglow XR, free. It is also open source, so you can build it yourself and put it on your own iPhone with Xcode.

Do I need the printed viewer?

For the headset experience, yes, or any Cardboard-style viewer with an open front for the cameras. Without one, phone view shows a single eye and lets you tap the screen to select what the centre cursor is on.

Where does the rhythm game get its tempo?

For tracks in your own library it analyses the audio on the phone. For Apple Music songs it looks the tempo up by title and artist, using audiometa.io, and tells you which source it used. If neither knows the song, you set the tempo yourself.

What about WebXR sites that need a headset?

Afterglow presents itself to pages as a headset with hand tracking. Sessions run with ultra-wide passthrough and head rotation; the page gets your hands as input sources.
