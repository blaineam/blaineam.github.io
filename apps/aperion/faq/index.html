Aperion

Frequently Asked Questions

How Aperion worked, what it ran on, and whether you can still get it.

Features

Yes. The star catalog and the pattern matcher are bundled inside the app and run on-device. There are no astrometry.net round-trips, no API keys, and no rate limits. Solves work on the mount's air-gapped Wi-Fi network at a remote dark site with no cellular signal.

The same algorithm as the astrometry.net project — the quad-hash pattern matcher with Bayesian odds-ratio scoring that backs the nova.astrometry.net web service and every serious astro tool that calls out to it. Aperion runs the core engine 100 % on-device.

The only substitution is the linear-algebra layer: astrometry.net's upstream build vendors GNU GSL (GPL v3), which isn't compatible with the App Store's distribution terms. Aperion swaps that subset for a permissively-licensed shim (3-clause BSD) backed by Apple's Accelerate framework — LAPACK for LU / QR / SVD, CBLAS for dense matrix multiply. Every component in the final binary ships under a permissive license; the full list is surfaced in the app's Settings → About & open source.

A traditional two-star alignment requires you to manually slew to known stars and confirm them. Aperion's auto-align is a closed loop: it captures a frame, plate-solves it offline to figure out exactly where the mount is pointing, syncs the mount to that coordinate, then slews the residual error toward your target. It repeats until the target is centered. You tap once and walk away.

As frames come in from the camera, Aperion detects stars, computes the translation between frames, aligns and integrates them on-device, and updates the build-up preview in real time. You see faint nebulosity emerge over the course of the session instead of waiting until you get home to find out you missed focus or had a bad guide.

Yes. Use the joystick to frame the top-left corner of the area you want, capture it, then frame the bottom-right corner and capture that too. Aperion computes the rectangular grid, applies your chosen overlap percentage, and walks the mount through every tile with the same exposure settings.

Optionally. By default the mount linearly interpolates between your start and end poses at the chosen frame rate and duration. Toggle sidereal tracking on and the mount counter-rotates earth's spin while panning, so the stars stay pinned in the frame and the foreground drifts beneath them.

The Sun, Moon, and planets are resolved against a live ephemeris instead of a stale catalog file, so their positions are always current. They appear in the target picker and the AR sky chart with visibility windows for tonight at your location.

Hardware

No. The mount-control features need a Polaris (over BLE + TCP), but Aperion still runs great on a plain tripod — you get capture, offline plate solving, live stacking, and the AR sky chart. The slew and goto controls simply grey themselves out until a compatible mount is connected.

Canon EOS bodies that support CCAPI (tested on the R5 Mark II with firmware 1.2) are the first-class path and connect over the mount's Wi-Fi hotspot. Other bodies — Nikon, Sony, Fuji, Olympus — connect via libgphoto2 over USB tether. The iPhone's own camera is also a supported capture source.

The Polaris exposes a Wi-Fi hotspot that your iPhone has to join in order to talk to the camera. That would normally cut your phone off from cellular and from the watch, but Aperion uses WatchConnectivity over Bluetooth so the watch keeps full control of the mount, the camera, and the plate-solve trigger regardless of what network the phone is on.

Status

No. It was planned as a one-time $9.99 universal purchase, but it never reached the App Store and it has been shelved — it isn't maintained and there's no build to install. If you want Polaris automation today, use the Benro Polaris Firmware Patcher instead. It's open source, it's free, and it runs on the mount itself.
