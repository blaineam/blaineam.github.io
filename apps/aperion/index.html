Yes. The star catalog and the pattern matcher are bundled inside the app and run on-device. There are no astrometry.net round-trips, no API keys, and no rate limits. Solves work on the mount's air-gapped Wi-Fi network at a remote dark site with no cellular signal.

A traditional two-star alignment requires you to manually slew to known stars and confirm them. Aperion's auto-align is a closed loop: it captures a frame, plate-solves it offline to figure out exactly where the mount is pointing, syncs the mount to that coordinate, then slews the residual error toward your target. It repeats until the target is centered. You tap once and walk away.

Canon EOS bodies that support CCAPI (tested on the R5 Mark II with firmware 1.2) are the first-class path and connect over the mount's Wi-Fi hotspot. Other bodies — Nikon, Sony, Fuji, Olympus — connect via libgphoto2 over USB tether. The iPhone's own camera is also a supported capture source.

The Polaris exposes a Wi-Fi hotspot that your iPhone has to join in order to talk to the camera. That would normally cut your phone off from cellular and from the watch, but Aperion uses WatchConnectivity over Bluetooth so the watch keeps full control of the mount, the camera, and the plate-solve trigger regardless of what network the phone is on.
