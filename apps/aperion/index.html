✦ Aperion

Deep-Sky Astrophotography, Shelved

One app drove your tracking mount, mirrorless camera, and offline plate solver — auto-align in seconds, live stacks, mosaic planning, and motion timelapses from an iPhone, iPad, Mac, or Apple Watch. It never shipped, and it isn't coming. This page stays up as the write-up.

Shelved · never released

Aperion drove a Benro Polaris mount and a Canon body from an unofficial, reverse-engineered control path. Getting that approved for the App Store — by Benro or by Apple — was never a realistic bet, and my Polaris is long out of warranty, so there was nothing left to protect by staying on the sanctioned side of the fence. Patching the mount's own firmware turned out to be the better fix: the automation runs on the device, so the Polaris can shoot all night without my phone tethered to it and without an app babysitting the session. That work lives on in the open-source Benro Polaris Firmware Patcher.

That means the loop closes in seconds even when you're tethered to the mount's air-gapped Wi-Fi hotspot — the only network you actually have at 11pm in a national forest.

Why Aperion Was Shelved

It was planned as a $9.99 universal purchase. It never shipped, and it isn't going to.

Not Available

Aperion talked to the Benro Polaris over a reverse-engineered BLE + TCP protocol. That is fine for a personal tool and a hard sell as a shipping product: the control path is undocumented, unsupported, and could break with any mount firmware update, and getting it sanctioned — by Benro, or through App Store review — was never a bet worth building a release around. Since my own Polaris is well past its warranty period, there was also nothing left to protect by staying on the sanctioned side of that line.

Patching the mount's firmware directly turned out to be the better workaround. The intelligence lives on the device rather than in a phone app, so the Polaris runs a full session — slews, dwells, shutter triggers — without my phone tethered to it and without an app driving it all night. No dead battery, no dropped BLE link at 3 a.m., no session lost because a phone locked up.

So Aperion is shelved: no longer maintained, never released, and removed from the apps directory. Its goal lives on in the open-source Benro Polaris Firmware Patcher, which is free and does the job better.

The same algorithm as the astrometry.net project — the quad-hash pattern matcher with Bayesian odds-ratio scoring that backs the nova.astrometry.net web service and every serious astro tool that calls out to it. Aperion runs the core engine 100 % on-device.

The only substitution is the linear-algebra layer: astrometry.net's upstream build vendors GNU GSL (GPL v3), which isn't compatible with the App Store's distribution terms. Aperion swaps that subset for a permissively-licensed shim (3-clause BSD) backed by Apple's Accelerate framework — LAPACK for LU / QR / SVD, CBLAS for dense matrix multiply. Every component in the final binary ships under a permissive license; the full list is surfaced in the app's Settings → About & open source.

A traditional two-star alignment requires you to manually slew to known stars and confirm them. Aperion's auto-align is a closed loop: it captures a frame, plate-solves it offline to figure out exactly where the mount is pointing, syncs the mount to that coordinate, then slews the residual error toward your target. It repeats until the target is centered. You tap once and walk away.

The Polaris exposes a Wi-Fi hotspot that your iPhone has to join in order to talk to the camera. That would normally cut your phone off from cellular and from the watch, but Aperion uses WatchConnectivity over Bluetooth so the watch keeps full control of the mount, the camera, and the plate-solve trigger regardless of what network the phone is on.

As frames come in from the camera, Aperion detects stars, computes the translation between frames, aligns and integrates them on-device, and updates the build-up preview in real time. You see faint nebulosity emerge over the course of the session instead of waiting until you get home to find out you missed focus or had a bad guide.
