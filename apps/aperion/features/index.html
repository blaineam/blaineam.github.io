Three Tools, One Source of Truth

No swapping between the Polaris app, Canon's control utility, and a plate-solving service. Aperion unified the entire capture loop.

Everything below describes the app as it was built. It was never released, and none of it is available to download — see why it's shelved.

  • 🎯

    Integrated Mount + Camera

    One screen drives the Benro Polaris mount over BLE + TCP and a Canon EOS body over CCAPI. Joystick, shutter, ISO wheels, and target picker all live together.

  • 🛰️

    Offline Plate Solving

    The same astrometry.net algorithm that powers every serious astrophotography tool, running 100 % on-device. Bundled star-index files, no internet, no API round-trips — solves finish in under a second on the mount's air-gapped Wi-Fi.

  • 🎯

    Auto-Align

    Shoot → solve → sync → nudge, on repeat, until your target is centered. No two-star alignment walk. Sun, Moon, and planets resolved via live ephemeris.

  • 🌌

    Live Stacking

    Translation-aligned integration with on-device star detection. The build-up preview updates every frame so you can watch faint nebulosity emerge in real time.

  • 🧩

    Mosaic Planner

    Set two corners from the joystick and Aperion walks the rectangular grid for you with configurable overlap. Capture wide-field panels of the Milky Way without doing the trig.

  • 🎬

    Motion Timelapses

    Linear pan between start and end poses at your chosen frame rate. Optional sidereal tracking pins the stars while the foreground drifts.

  • 📱

    AR Sky Chart

    Point your phone at the sky and see labeled targets overlaid via ARKit. Tap a galaxy or nebula to slew the mount and start the capture.

  • ⌚

    Apple Watch Companion

    Full mount joystick, camera-setting wheels, plate-solve trigger, and a zoomable preview — right on your wrist. Works over Bluetooth even when your phone is on the mount's hotspot.

  • 🖥️

    Mac on the Big Canvas

    Same source tree, same controls, larger workspace. Review the night's gallery, reprocess RAW frames, and rebuild stacks at desktop scale.

From Tap to Framed in Seconds

The closed loop that replaces the two-star alignment walk most mounts still demand.

  1. 1

    Pick a target

    Browse the catalog, the AR sky chart, or visible-tonight filtered by altitude.

  2. 2

    Capture a frame

    Aperion triggers the camera over CCAPI and pulls the JPEG preview down the network.

  3. 3

    Solve offline

    The bundled star-pattern matcher figures out exactly where the camera is pointing — no internet required.

  4. 4

    Sync & nudge

    The mount syncs to the solved coordinate and slews the residual error. Loop until the target is centered.

  • 🛰️

    Plate Solving That Actually Works in the Field

    Most plate-solving workflows assume you have a reliable internet connection at your dark site. You don't. Aperion ships the catalog and the pattern matcher inside the app and runs the solve on the device's neural and CPU cores.

    That means the loop closes in seconds even when you're tethered to the mount's air-gapped Wi-Fi hotspot — the only network you actually have at 11pm in a national forest.

  • ⌚

    The Watch You Already Wear, Now in Charge

    Walk away from the tripod. The Apple Watch companion gives you the full mount joystick, ISO and shutter wheels, lens picker, framing-corner capture, plate-solve trigger, and a zoomable live preview of both the raw viewfinder and the in-progress stack.

    Pairing happens automatically over WatchConnectivity even when the phone is joined to the mount's offline Wi-Fi network — a surprisingly hard problem that Aperion just handles.

  • 🧩

    Mosaics & Motion, Without the Spreadsheet

    Frame your two corners from the joystick. Aperion computes the tile grid, the overlap, and walks the mount through every panel — capturing each frame with the same exposure ladder.

    For motion timelapses, set a start pose and an end pose, pick a duration and frame rate, and let the linear interpolator drive the mount. Toggle sidereal tracking to keep the stars pinned while the landscape rolls beneath them.

Why Aperion Was Shelved

It was planned as a $9.99 universal purchase. It never shipped, and it isn't going to.

  • App Store approval was never a realistic bet
  • Mount control relied on an unofficial protocol
  • Neither Benro nor Apple had reason to bless it
  • My Polaris is out of warranty — nothing to risk
  • A firmware patch solves it on the device instead
  • The mount can now shoot all night unattended
  • That work is open source and free

Aperion talked to the Benro Polaris over a reverse-engineered BLE + TCP protocol. That is fine for a personal tool and a hard sell as a shipping product: the control path is undocumented, unsupported, and could break with any mount firmware update, and getting it sanctioned — by Benro, or through App Store review — was never a bet worth building a release around. Since my own Polaris is well past its warranty period, there was also nothing left to protect by staying on the sanctioned side of that line.

Patching the mount's firmware directly turned out to be the better workaround. The intelligence lives on the device rather than in a phone app, so the Polaris runs a full session — slews, dwells, shutter triggers — without my phone tethered to it and without an app driving it all night. No dead battery, no dropped BLE link at 3 a.m., no session lost because a phone locked up.

So Aperion is shelved: no longer maintained, never released, and removed from the apps directory. Its goal lives on in the open-source Benro Polaris Firmware Patcher, which is free and does the job better.
