Luma Editor

A starting point, not a finish line.

Most photo apps sell you a look and stop there. You tap a filter, you get a picture, and the only thing left to decide is how much of it you want.

Luma works the other way round. Point it at a photograph you admire — your own, or one by a photographer you are learning from — and it studies how that picture treats light and colour, then solves for the sliders that bring your photo there. What you get back is the recipe. Warm it further, pull the highlights down, lift only the blues. It is your edit from the first tap.

Match

Borrow the night you wish you'd shot.

Here Luma was shown a vivid Milky Way photograph and a flat, muddy night shot, and asked to make the second look like the first. Nothing below was retouched by hand.

Match a whole shoot, or just the subject.

  • A library of looks. Save the photos you admire as styles, and develop an afternoon's work at once — each picture is matched to the style that suits it best.
  • Region against region. Match a style inside a mask, so your subject takes on their subject's look and the background is left alone.
  • Presets, copy and paste. Keep a look as a named preset or paste one photo's edit onto the next. Framing stays with the photo it was drawn on.

Develop

Everything a darkroom had. Nothing baked in.

The editor speaks the vocabulary of the Photos app and goes further. Every change is a parameter, so a photo reopened next year comes back with every slider exactly where you left it.

  • Light, Color, Detail, Effects. Exposure, Brilliance, Highlights, Shadows, Contrast, Black and White Point, Vibrance, Warmth, Tint, Sharpness, Definition, Noise Reduction, Vignette and Grain.
  • Curves and colour. Four-channel tone curves, an eight-band colour mixer, split toning and a proper black-and-white conversion.
  • RAW, developed properly. RAW captures are developed from sensor data, with the full highlight headroom the file carries.
  • Auto Enhance, and undo. One tap gives you a starting point, and undo and redo go a step at a time — a whole slider drag counts as one.
  • Intensity that means something. It scales the adjustments themselves, so 60% is every slider at 60% — not a faded picture laid over the original.
  • Masks. Linear and radial gradients you drag on the photo, luminance and colour ranges, and Subject, Background and People found on device — each with its own full set of adjustments.
  • Crop, straighten, perspective. A thirds grid and aspect presets, a horizon found automatically, and verticals put right on a building shot from below.
  • What the glass did. Lens distortion, chromatic aberration and colour fringing, corrected.

Retouch

Tap a blemish. Move the repair next month.

Luma rebuilds a spot from the texture already around it — assembled from many samples, so a repair follows an edge instead of stamping over it. Switch to Clone to choose the source yourself.

  • Stored as a parameter. Every spot can be moved, resized or removed long after, like any other slider.
  • Honest by design. Luma only ever uses what is in the frame. Taking a whole object out means inventing what was behind it — that is a job for Ari Helper, one tap away.

Cull

Forty frames of the same moment. One keeper.

Hand Luma a burst or a whole day. It groups what belongs to the same moment and judges each frame the way a person would — then suggests the keeper, and says so when it is a close call.

  • Is it sharp where it matters?

    Focus is measured on the subject — a face, an eye, an animal — not the background.

  • Are the eyes open?

    For people and for pets, from the eyes themselves rather than a guess about the face.

  • Did anything move?

    Blur from movement is measured on the subject itself, where it spoils a frame.

  • Is it composed?

    Where the subject sits in the frame, weighed alongside everything else.

Page through any group full size and overrule it. Picks are filed into a Luma Keepers album, best first, and sent straight to the editor. Nothing is ever deleted.

Inside Photos

Your library stays your library.

  • Non-destructive. The original is never touched, Revert to Original always works, and edits sync through iCloud Photos.
  • In the Photos app. Tap Edit, then the extensions button, then Luma — the whole editor, your styles and presets, on a photo or a video.
  • Video too. Framing, tone, colour, curves and gradient and range masks apply to every frame of a clip.
  • Take it elsewhere. Export an edit as Camera Raw settings for Lightroom, or as a real 3D LUT for Final Cut Pro, Premiere Pro and DaVinci Resolve.

Better together

Luma develops. Ari imagines.

Luma never invents anything that was not in the photograph. When you need something gone — a leash, a stranger, a sign — Luma hands the picture to Ari Helper's Photo Studio and takes it back with your grade intact.

  • Luma Editor

    The developing room. Every edit is a parameter you can read back and reverse — true to the photograph you took.

  • Ari Helper

    The studio. On-device models that remove objects, repair damaged prints, add colour, enlarge, take out noise and camera shake, and clean up old film.

    See what Ari can do →

  1. Retouch in Luma. Open the Retouch tab on the photo you are grading.
  2. Remove Something in Ari. Luma sends the untouched photo, at full size, to Ari's Photo Studio.
  3. Brush it out. Paint over what should go; Ari tightens the stroke onto the object and fills it in.
  4. Save to Luma Editor. You land back where you were, with the object gone and every slider still set.
