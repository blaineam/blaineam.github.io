Match

Luma measures how the photo you picked actually looks — its tonal distribution, its colour balance, how it treats each family of hues, what it does in the shadows and the highlights — and then solves for the adjustments that move your photo there. The answer comes back as sliders, curves and a colour mixer you can go on editing, not as a finished picture.

That is the point of it. Every match lands as ordinary adjustments — Light, Color, Detail, Effects, tone curves, the colour mixer, split toning — and you can change any of them, add masks, crop, retouch, and undo step by step. The Intensity control scales the adjustments themselves rather than fading a finished picture over your original, so the sliders stay meaningful at any setting.

Yes. Develop a whole shoot at once and Luma matches each photo to the closest style, so the results are consistent without one preset being forced onto pictures it does not suit. Save a look as a named preset, or copy a look from one photo and paste it onto the next.

Develop

Tap a blemish and it goes. Luma finds clean skin or sky nearby to replace it with, and you can drag the source somewhere else if you disagree. Spots are stored as adjustments rather than painted into the picture, so a repair can be moved, resized or removed months later like any other slider.

It handles the part of a burst nobody enjoys. Luma groups the near-duplicates, measures each frame for focus, face capture quality and clipping, and suggests which one to keep — then files the picks into a Luma Keepers album, best first, so you can find them in Photos afterwards. Every pick is a suggestion you can change, and nothing is ever deleted.

LUTs (Lookup Tables) are colour grading presets that other apps can read. Luma generates a real 3D LUT (.cube) from your edit by running an identity lattice through its own renderer, so what you export matches what is on screen — ready for Final Cut Pro, Premiere Pro and DaVinci Resolve. You can also export as Camera Raw XMP for Lightroom. Neither format can carry masks or retouching, and Luma tells you when an export is leaving something behind.

When both apps are installed, Luma's Retouch tab offers Remove Something in Ari. Luma sends the untouched photo to Ari's Photo Studio, you remove what you need with Ari's on-device models, and Save to Luma Editor brings you back with the object gone and your grade still applied. Luma itself never invents content; that part happens in Ari, where it is visible.
